When I visited India over the holidays, jet lag and the heat would sometimes wake me before dawn. In the dark, I’d watch my paati clutch her knees and hold her breath as she slowly pulled herself up from the floor, trying to manage the pain of her arthritis. Seeing her quietly wince, I’d urge her to sleep a little longer, but she’d wave me off.

Hobbling outside, she’d begin her day by drawing a kolam.

Paati drew a kolam every day for nearly sixty years, from the day she got married. Her dots were perfectly spaced, her lines confident, her movements swift. She drew even the most intricate kolams effortlessly. But I don’t think she ever thought of herself as an artist. When she finished, she’d move on to her next task without so much as a glance back at what she had created.

Last year, Paati told me she’d had to stop drawing kolams outside the house because her arthritis had gotten worse. I was grateful I had filmed her the year before, and I asked if she wanted to see it.
